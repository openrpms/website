Applications

RPMS holds the data and the core logic. The applications are the interfaces and views people actually work in. Each one asks the server for what it needs, almost always through RPCs, so the same record is shared by everyone who opens it.

Here are the main ones, who works in each, and what to know before you reuse it. Which of them work with a release built from the public files is covered in detail on How a release is built.

VueCentric, the chart

The Windows desktop where clinicians review the patient, write notes, and place orders. It hosts the parts of the chart, such as the cover sheet, problems, medications, and notes, in one window.

Who works in it
Providers, nurses, medical assistants, and coders
Connects through
The CIA broker
Runs on
IRIS or YottaDB

Know this: a few commercial libraries the chart needs are not in the public files. A clinic with a licensed IHS install already has them.

iCare, the patient panel

The program for looking across a group of patients, rather than one chart at a time. It builds panels by provider, condition, or criteria, and shows who is due for care and who needs follow-up.

Who works in it
Care and case managers
Connects through
The BMX broker
Runs on
IRIS or YottaDB

Know this: the server side of iCare is in the release. The desktop program is a separate IHS download.

Behavioral Health

The Behavioral Health System keeps behavioral health records in the same chart as the rest of the clinic, while limiting who can read them. Notes are written under behavioral health titles, and only the clinicians allowed to see them can open them. Other providers can see that a note exists without seeing what it says.

Who works in it
Behavioral health providers
Where
Inside VueCentric, and its own text screens
Why it matters
Substance use records are protected by 42 CFR Part 2

Keep behavioral health records private is the use case it serves.

BPRM, the front desk

Practice management: registration, scheduling, and billing, in a web application that runs on a Windows server.

Who works in it
Front desk, scheduling, benefits, and admissions
Connects through
The database directly, through BMW, not RPCs
Runs on
IRIS only

Know this: BPRM needs an IRIS license and IHS's installer. On YottaDB, registration and scheduling go through the server's own interfaces instead.

lakeraven-ehr, on the web

A web front end for RPMS, open source under the MIT license. It runs in a browser, reads and writes through the same RPCs the desktop programs use, and keeps no clinical data of its own. RPMS stays the system of record.

Who works in it
Clinic staff, from a browser
Connects through
The CIA or XWB broker, using the rpms-rpc library
Runs on
IRIS or YottaDB

Try it: the public demo at demo.openrpms.org runs lakeraven-ehr on a release built from the public files. The source is at github.com/lakeraven/lakeraven-ehr.

Building a new application? Start with RPCs for what the server answers, and Users & use cases for who you are building it for. The server's own packages are listed on Packages.
